# IRA CARE: market evidence (UK and England)

Compiled 26 November 2026 from desk research. This is secondary evidence about the size and
shape of the need IRA CARE is designed for. It is **not** evidence that anyone wants IRA CARE:
no customer has been interviewed, no pilot has run and no price has been tested
(`docs/evidence/ASSUMPTIONS_REGISTER.md`, section 1). Use it to frame questions, not to answer
them.

## How to read this document

Every figure carries one of two labels.

- **Verified at source.** Read on the publisher's own page or document on 26 November 2026.
  From the build environment only `www.gov.uk` and `assets.publishing.service.gov.uk` could be
  opened, so every verified figure comes from a government statistical release or a document
  laid before Parliament.
- **Unverified.** Seen only in a search-engine summary of the publisher's page, or quoted by a
  third party. The publisher's own page could not be opened (see "Sources that could not be
  reached" at the end). Re-read each one at source before it goes into a plan, a deck or an
  application.

Geography matters. Most social care statistics are for **England only**; population and
living-alone figures are often for **England and Wales** or the **UK**. Each row says which.
Figures from different years, geographies and definitions must not be added together or
compared directly.

**What this means for IRA CARE.** The evidence for long lies is real but thin and dated, and none
of it measures whether a proactive check-in shortens the time to help. Any claim that IRA CARE
reduces long lies would need a pilot with an agreed measure; it cannot be made today.

## 10. What the evidence supports, and what it does not

Supported by verified sources:

- the number of older people in England, and the large number who live alone (P1, P2; P4 and
  P5 unverified);
- sustained demand on councils, with most requests ending without long-term support (D1 to D3);
- a growing, fragmenting homecare market and a care workforce with persistent vacancies,
  especially in homecare (H2 to H4, W1, W2);
- falls are common and a leading cause of emergency admission among older people (F1 to F3);
- a hard national deadline (end of January 2027 for most lines) that forces telecare users and
  councils to replace or reconfigure analogue equipment (T2, T3).

Not supported by anything in this document:

- that older people, families, care providers or councils would use or pay for IRA CARE;
- that proactive check-ins reduce falls, long lies, admissions or care worker time;
- any market size in pounds for IRA CARE. A top-down number built from these figures would be
  an assumption, and must be labelled as one (`ASSUMPTIONS_FOR_PROJECTIONS.md`).
